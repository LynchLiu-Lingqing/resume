LLM-ASSISTED ERP · 2025–2026

AI that fits the organization.

Cross-border e-commerce operations depend on continuous monitoring, coordination, and data entry. Overseas sales add time-zone pressure. A useful recommendation has to account for cash flow, supplier lead times, inventory policies, and local conditions.

MY ROLEProduct & business lead
PROJECT SCALE / OUTCOME10 firms adopted the system5-person engineering team

My role

I worked across client discovery, workflow mapping, product design, development coordination, and testing. I helped secure support for the initial build, earned access to business workflows, and translated between owners, employees, and a five-person engineering team.

What we built

The team delivered sales monitoring, operational issue detection, inventory-risk forecasting, and SKU-related actions. A domain knowledge base combined company data with industry knowledge and operating context. Consequential actions remained behind approval steps.

What changed

Ten cross-border e-commerce firms adopted the product. The central design challenge was to make recommendations useful under different business conditions while retaining human review.

Designing for different firms

Working across firms made the importance of company-specific decision rules clear. Similar businesses could have different approval structures, cash constraints, and risk tolerance. That experience shaped my approach to product design: distinguish shared functions from local operating rules, and define approval boundaries and acceptance criteria with the people using the system.

The open question

How can a system adapt without becoming unpredictable? I want to understand when it should reuse past knowledge, update its assumptions, or defer to a person—and how employees can build capability through using it.
