RESEARCH NETWORKS · 2024–2026

How knowledge gets organized.

How do humanities and social science laboratories form, collaborate, and connect knowledge across disciplinary and institutional boundaries? The project studies research organizations as knowledge-production conditions change.

MY ROLEResearch assistant & first author
LABORATORY RESEARCH DATASET250+ laboratories8,000+ scholars

My contribution

I built a dataset covering more than 250 laboratories and 8,000 scholars and used network analysis to study collaboration and institutional connections. For the paper on laboratories in the United States, I identified the research problem, established the database, developed the analytical framework, conducted analysis, and wrote the manuscript.

Research context

This work formed part of Humanities and Social Science Laboratory Studies, a National Social Science Fund project (24&ZD292). My research assistantship ran from February 2024 to August 2026.

Output

Reorganizing knowledge production: The organizational forms of humanities and social science laboratories in the United States. Presented at the 2026 Annual Conference of the Chinese Sociological Association, Sociology of Technology session; first author with Peng Lü.

What I take forward

Entering an unfamiliar field starts with understanding its roles, boundaries, incentives, and external ties. I use the same approach in product work: understand how an industry is organized before deciding what a technology can realistically change.
